How Much to Pay Kids for Chores (With an Allowance Tracker That Works)

By Kidnova · Updated November 26, 2026

If you have decided to pay your children for chores, the next question is always: how much? There is no single right amount, because it depends on your budget, your child's age and what the money is for. What helps most is a clear, fair system your child understands, and an allowance tracker they can see every day. Here is a simple way to set it up.

1. Decide which chores are paid

Many families keep everyday family jobs unpaid, like tidying toys or clearing their own plate, and pay only for extra jobs, like washing the car or helping in the garden. Decide this first and explain it to your child, so there are no surprises later. Not sure which jobs fit your child's age? See our chores by age chart.

2. Start from a weekly budget

Think about the most you are happy to pay in one week, then work backwards. Starting from your budget means you never promise more than you can pay, and you can always raise prices later. Some families use a simple rule of thumb, like a set amount for each year of age per week, but any amount works if it is the same every week.

3. Price chores by effort

Give bigger or harder jobs a higher price than quick ones. A two-minute job should not pay the same as a job that takes half an hour. Writing the price next to each chore makes the system clear and stops arguments about fairness.

4. Agree on what "done" means

Show your child how each chore should look when it is finished. If the job is not done, it is not paid, but let them fix it and try again. This teaches that money is earned by finishing the job, not by starting it.

5. Track it on an allowance tracker and pay on the same day

A kids allowance tracker is simply a chart with each chore, its price and a box for every day of the week. Your child ticks each job when it is done, adds up the daily total, then you add the week together on one fixed day. Paying on the same day every week, like a real payday, builds trust and teaches children to plan. Older kids can do the adding themselves, which is great maths practice.

6. Decide what the money is for

Agree on what your child will pay for with their own money, for example small toys or treats. This is where the real learning happens: they start to compare prices and choose.

Help them save part of it

Encourage your child to put part of every payday aside for a bigger goal. Seeing savings grow toward something they really want teaches patience better than any lecture. Our guide on how to teach kids about money has more ideas.

Final thoughts

The exact amount matters less than a system that is clear, fair and the same every week. Start small, keep the rules simple and adjust as your child grows.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I pay my child for chores?

There is no single right amount. Start from a weekly budget you are comfortable with, give bigger jobs a higher price than quick ones, and keep the prices the same every week.

What is a kids allowance tracker?

It is a chart that lists each chore, its price and a box for every day. Kids tick off finished jobs, add up the daily total and get paid on the same day each week.

Should everyday chores be paid?

Many families keep everyday family jobs unpaid, like tidying toys, and pay only for extra jobs. Decide this first and explain it to your child.

What day should I pay my child?

Pick one fixed day every week, like Friday or Sunday. A regular payday builds trust and helps children plan their spending and saving.

At what age can kids start earning money for chores?

Many families start around ages 5 to 7, when children can count, follow a weekly chart and understand that money is earned by finishing a job.
