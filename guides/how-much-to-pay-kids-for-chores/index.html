How Much to Pay Kids for Chores: A Simple Way to Set Prices

By Kidnova · Updated November 2026

If you have decided to pay your children for chores, the next question is always: how much? There is no single right amount, because it depends on your budget, your child's age and what the money is for. What helps most is a clear, fair system your child understands. Here is a simple way to set it up.

1. Decide which chores are paid

Many families keep everyday family jobs unpaid, like tidying toys or clearing their own plate, and pay only for extra jobs, like washing the car or helping in the garden. Decide this first and explain it to your child, so there are no surprises later.

2. Start from a weekly budget

Think about the most you are happy to pay in one week, then work backwards. Starting from your budget means you never promise more than you can pay, and you can always raise prices later.

3. Price chores by effort

Give bigger or harder jobs a higher price than quick ones. A two-minute job should not pay the same as a job that takes half an hour. Writing the price next to each chore makes the system clear and stops arguments about fairness.

4. Agree on what "done" means

Show your child how each chore should look when it is finished. If the job is not done, it is not paid, but let them fix it and try again. This teaches that money is earned by finishing the job, not by starting it.

5. Track it and pay on the same day

Let your child tick off each chore on a chart during the week, then add it up together on one fixed day. Paying on the same day every week, like a real payday, builds trust and teaches children to plan.

6. Decide what the money is for

Agree on what your child will pay for with their own money, for example small toys or treats. This is where the real learning happens: they start to compare prices and choose.

Help them save part of it

Encourage your child to put part of every payday aside for a bigger goal. Seeing savings grow toward something they really want teaches patience better than any lecture.

Final thoughts

The exact amount matters less than a system that is clear, fair and the same every week. Start small, keep the rules simple and adjust as your child grows.
