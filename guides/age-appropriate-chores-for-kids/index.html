Age-Appropriate Chores for Kids: Chores by Age Chart (Ages 2 to 10)

By Kidnova · Updated November 26, 2026

Chores are not only about a tidy house. When children help at home, they learn that they are capable and that the family counts on them. The key is choosing age-appropriate chores, so they can succeed on their own. Below is a quick chores by age chart, then a closer look at each age, plus tips that make chores a habit instead of a daily argument.

Chores by age chart

AgeAge-appropriate chores
2 to 3Put toys in a basket, throw rubbish in the bin, put dirty clothes in the laundry basket, wipe small spills, help feed a pet
4 to 5Make their bed, set napkins and spoons, water plants, match socks, put books back, clear their own plate, dust low shelves
6 to 7Pack their school bag, set and clear the table, fold small laundry, tidy their room, feed pets, put away groceries, sweep with a small broom
8 to 10Vacuum a room, help cook simple meals, take out the rubbish, sort and put away their own laundry, wash dishes, plan their homework and day

Every child is different. Use the chart as a starting point and move up or down a row depending on what your child can do safely.

Ages 2 to 3: little helpers

Toddlers love to copy adults, so turn that into help. Good first responsibilities: put toys in a basket, throw rubbish in the bin, put dirty clothes in the laundry basket, wipe up small spills and help feed a pet with an adult. Expect to do it together at this age, and keep each job to a minute or two.

Ages 4 to 5: simple daily jobs

Preschoolers can do short tasks on their own: make their bed (it does not need to be perfect), set napkins and spoons on the table, water plants, match socks, put books back on the shelf and clear their own plate after meals. A picture chart helps, because many children this age cannot read yet. A sticker for every finished job makes the routine feel like a game.

Ages 6 to 7: a real routine

Children this age can follow a weekly list. Try: pack their school bag, set and clear the table, fold small laundry, tidy their room, feed pets and help put away the shopping. A weekly chart with 5 to 7 chores is enough. Let them tick each one off themselves, so they own the routine.

Ages 8 to 10: bigger responsibilities

Older children can vacuum a room, help cook simple meals, take out the rubbish, wash dishes, sort their own laundry and plan their day. This is also a good age to plan homework and activities on paper, and to start linking extra jobs with pocket money if your family wants to.

How many chores should a child do?

Less than most parents think. Two or three short daily jobs are plenty for toddlers and preschoolers. School-age children can handle three to five daily jobs plus one bigger weekly job, like tidying their room or helping with the laundry. A short list that gets done every day teaches more than a long list that gets ignored.

What if my child refuses?

Resistance is normal, especially when a chore is new. Keep your tone calm and matter-of-fact, offer a small choice (do you want to feed the cat or water the plants first?), and work side by side for the first few days. A visual chart helps too: children argue less with a chart than with a parent, because the plan is already decided.

Tips that make chores stick

Show how to do each job the first few times. Put the chart where your child can see it, and praise effort, not perfection. Do chores at the same time each day, for example right after breakfast, so they become part of the routine. Rotate one job every few weeks so chores do not get boring.

Should kids get paid for chores?

Families do this differently, and both ways can work. Many parents keep everyday family jobs unpaid, then offer money for extra chores. If you want to connect chores and money, a pay per chore chart makes it clear and fair. Read our guide on how much to pay kids for chores for a simple way to set prices.

Final thoughts

Start small, stay consistent and celebrate progress. A child who feeds the cat every morning at 5 is learning responsibility that will help them for years.
