12 Screen-Free Winter Activities for Toddlers and Preschoolers

By Kidnova · Updated November 2026

Cold days, early sunsets and a lot of time indoors: winter can feel long with a toddler. The good news is that little ones do not need much to stay busy. These 12 screen-free activities use things you already have at home, take a few minutes to set up, and quietly practice skills like counting, colors, focus and fine motor control.

1. Color by number

Color by number pages are a calm activity that teaches number and color matching at the same time. For ages 3 to 5, choose pages with big spaces and only 2 or 3 colors so your child can finish on their own.

2. Toy car parking game

Draw numbered parking spots on paper or use a printable mat, then ask your child to park each car in the right spot. It is counting practice that feels like play, and it works for a wide range of ages.

Car Play Mat Printable – Road Number Matching GameNumbered spots from 1 to 8, sized for real toy cars.

3. Indoor snowball toss

Roll socks into balls and toss them into a laundry basket. Move the basket further back as your child gets better, and count the snowballs together at the end.

4. Sticky wall art

Tape a sheet of contact paper to the wall, sticky side out, and let your child press on cotton balls, paper shapes or tissue paper to make a snowman or a winter scene. No glue, no mess.

5. Picture puzzles

Simple logic games build thinking skills. Picture sudoku is a great first step for 4 to 6 year olds, because children place pictures instead of numbers.

Picture Sudoku: Cut and Paste Logic Puzzles33 cut and paste picture puzzles for ages 4 to 6.

6. Sock sorting by color

Dump a basket of clean socks on the floor and sort them by color, or find the pairs. It is a real chore, a matching game and a color lesson in one.

7. Pillow obstacle course

Lay pillows, cushions and a blanket tunnel across the floor. Children crawl, step and jump through the course, which burns energy on days when you cannot go outside.

8. Playdough snowmen

Roll three balls of white playdough into a snowman and add buttons, sticks and a scarf. Rolling and pinching strengthens the small hand muscles children need for writing later.

9. Build a reading fort

Throw a blanket over two chairs, add a flashlight and a pile of books. Reading in a fort turns a normal story time into a small adventure.

10. Little baker

Let your child help with simple baking: pouring, stirring and counting spoonfuls. Cooking together is full of numbers, new words and patience.

11. Ice and warm water play

Freeze small toys in a container of water, then give your child warm water and a spoon to rescue them. Put a towel underneath and always stay close during water play.

12. Games for two

If you have two children at home, face-to-face games keep them busy together. Alphabet mazes and tic-tac-toe are great for ages 5 to 8.

Duo Game Book: Alphabet Mazes & Tic-Tac-ToeAlphabet maze races and tic-tac-toe for two players.

Final thoughts

You do not need to plan every minute. Pick two or three ideas for the day, leave some time for free play, and keep small objects like buttons away from children under 3. A little structure is often all it takes to make a long winter day feel calm and fun.
